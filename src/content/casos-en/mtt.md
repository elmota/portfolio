## In one sentence

I took a procedure that only existed at the branch office to a digital portal, validating with the same people who used to have to go in person, and I left a design system so the rest of the MTT's procedures could migrate with the same criteria.

## Context

The Ministry of Transport and Telecommunications offered **transport permit** procedures —for individuals and for private companies— only in person at a branch office. The goal of the project was to migrate that service to a 100% digital portal, keeping the trust and clarity that a government procedure requires.

## The design problem

It wasn't about digitizing a paper form. We had to understand who the branch office serves today, which questions a person resolves at the counter that a portal doesn't resolve on its own, and design a flow that doesn't abandon people who aren't used to digital procedures, with authentication through Clave Única.

## Key decisions

1. **Discovery started with people, not with the internal process.** Understanding how someone does the procedure today at the branch office before designing its digital version.
2. **Validating with user testing, not just with the Ministry's team.** The flow hypotheses were tested with the same people who would use the portal.
3. **A shared design system.** So a procedure digitized today doesn't end up as a visual island next to the rest of the portal.
4. **UX inside the implementation team.** I worked on integrating the prototypes with the Angular development team, so the design wouldn't get lost when it was built.

## What I delivered

- **Discovery** of the procedure and its current users at the branch office.
- **User testing** on the proposed flows.
- **Design system** for the portal.
- **Prototypes** of the complete flow, including authentication with Clave Única.
- **Support during implementation** in Angular.
